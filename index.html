Travelling the world with the Celiac disease

A brief guide on where to eat while travelling with Celiac disease.
We already struggled so why should you?

  • Explore!

Hello There!

We are a couple that loves to travel but really cannot stand gluten. To make it easier, one of us is even vegetarian!
So since we spend a considerable amount of time planning our trips around our meals, we thought it would be helpul to share some tips and give some recommendations.
This is hopefully better than going around playing trick or treat where you get nice food but you don't know what your intestine has in store for you...
  ... if you know, you know.

Tips:

  • Before going to places, we always look up restaurants in the FindMe GF app. You can search for restaurants by location and you will see ratings and reviews from other celiac travelers. This is a real life saver!
  • We and our family members have found it useful to take Gluteo Stop: a tablet that might prevent you from getting glutened by cross-contamination. It is available in Europe without prescription.
  • If you are visiting a country whose language you don't speak, it is a good idea to have a translation app and a card with your dietary requirements.
  • This might sound obvious, but if you are travelling to East-Asia, we recommend bringing your own bottle of soy sauce.

Notes:

  • For each country, we will provide a list of restaurants that we have personally tried, detailing what we ate, how good it was, and how safe we felt.
  • We try to prioritize restaurants that are explicitly labeled as gluten-free and vegetarian. If gluten is your only constraint, than restaurants offering meat or fish are always an option (careful with the sauces, sides, and seasoning).
  • The rankings are city specific, meaning that a maximum of 5/5 is the best you can find in that specific city.
