Overview

We travelled to the U.S. for a work event in LA and we planned a road trip focused on the national parks in the Southwest.
We landed in LA and queued for almost 3 hours at the passport control. Then, we used one of the free shuttle services to get to the car rental office.
Overall, we spent a couple of days in LA, then one night in Las Vegas, a couple of days in Zion National Park, and two nights in the Grand Canyon North Rim. Finally, we drove back to Pasadena (LA) and past Las Vegas for some shopping.

The US are definitely a country for meat lovers, and as such gluten is not a too big concern. Also, although this might be an outdated Italian trend, there are Hard Rock Cafés in the major cities which also offer a few gluten-free options.
However, we were still able to find some vegetarian options along the way, although in many instances we preferred to get some healthy snacks from the Walmart.

Los Angeles

To be honest, we were a bit concerned at first about the crime rate in LA, so we opted for one of the safer neighborhoods. This didn't end up being a problem, since you have to take the car and drive to places regardless of where you stay. We ended up booking the DoubleTree by Hilton Whittier Los Angeles which included parking and offered us some gluten-free cookies.
We visited the Griffith Observatory, the Hollywood Walk of Fame, Beverly Hills, the Santa Monica Pier, and Point Dume.
The Observatory is a must-see, even more so if you are passionate about astronomy. Being on a hill, it offers a great view of the city and the Hollywood sign. Since it can be crowded, we arrived there close to the opening time and we found parking in the lower lot, to then walk up to the Observatory. We highly recommend to watch one of the shows in the theater, which require a separate ticket.
The Hollywood Walk of Fame is a bit overrated, and we were not too impressed.
A special mention goes to Point Dume, which we reached just to drive through Malibu. If you manage to find parking, you can take a small walk to some observation decks to enjoy the scenery of the coastline. Most surprisingle, you can also spot some seals and sea lions on the rocks below.

Where to Eat in Los Angeles

  • Veggie Grill. This place was definitely our favorite. We probably went there almost every day and never got tired of it. Sure thing, it is on the unhealthier side, but it is a great option for a quick and tasty meal. We tried both the one in Hollywood and the one in Pasadena.
    Note: this is a chain that featured many locations in LA. However, sadly most of them closed down.

    Ratings:
    • 5/5: very many options, among which all the burgers and some burritos.
    • 5/5: everything is vegetarian-friendly and really delicious, so no concerns here.
  • The Green Leaf Thai Cuisine. A Thai restaurant with a few gluten-free and vegetarian options. The staff is very nice and will help you find something suitable. It is at 6 minutes walking distance from the DoubleTree by Hilton Whittier Los Angeles, and the yellow curry was very tasty.

    Ratings:
    • 3/5: not amazing but considering the non-turistic location it's a pretty good option.
    • 3/5: some vegetarian options available especially aomng the curries.

Zion National Park

Zion National Park is a truly beautiful place, which offers stunning hikes, among which the Angels Landing and the Narrows. Our original plan was to hike the Narrows one day and Angels Landing the next day, but a storm prevented us from doing Angels Landing.
Still, the Narrows is probably one of the most beautiful hikes we have ever done, and we highly recommend it. You can reach the Zion Canyon Visitor Center by car (a parking fee applies). There, you can rent the necessary equipment for the Narrows, such as the neoprene socks, boots, and the walking stick. Then, a free shuttle will take you to the starting point of the hike.
Since the hike is inside the Canyon and for most part in the river (up to our hips when we did it), it is important to check the weather forecast and the water level before starting the hike.
Also, you might need to get a permit for both hikes, so make sure to check the official website of the park for more information.
We stayed in Hurricane, a small town just outside the park which offers a few accommodation options. We booked a room at the Super 8 by Wyndham, which is a budget hotel good enough for a couple of nights.

Where to Eat in Hurricane

  • Siam Sapp Thai Cuisine. This Thai restaurant offers a few gluten-free and vegetarian options. The staff is very nice and will accommodate your needs.
    I don't know if we were just extremely hungry after the hike, but we remember the food to be very tasty and the portions quite generous.

    Ratings:
    • 4/5: good gluten-free options, and thai food is always great
    • 3/5: limited but acceptable choices.

Grand Canyon North Rim

We decided to visit the North Rim of the Grand Canyon because it is less crowded that the South Rim, and it was also closer to Zion National Park.
We recommend to book a cabin at the Grand Canyon Lodge, which is the only accommodation available in the North Rim.
We stayed in one of the inner / cheaper cabins, but the main part of the lodge, hosting a cozy living room and a restaurant, is the perfect spot to enjoy the breathtaking views of the canyon at any time of the day.
Most of the viewpoints of the North Rim are accessible by car, but there are also some hiking trails that allow you to get closer to the canyon and enjoy the views from different perspectives.

Note: We are heartbroken to hear that the Grand Canyon Lodge burned down in the 2025 fire.

Where to Eat in Grand Canyon North Rim

Besides being totally shocked by the views, we were also surprised that we could even eat at the lodge.
Probably the best idea though is not to rely too much on the food there and bring some sandwiches or snacks with you.

  • Deli in the Pines. Despite being something between a diner and a supermarket, it offers a burger with the gluten-free bun and the Beyond Meat patty. Alternatively, the beef brisket is a solid option. That was basically it!

    Ratings:
    • 4/5: not bad at all considering the location.
    • 3/5: only one option but very tasty.
  • Grand Canyon Lodge. We didn't try the restaurant at the lodge, but by talking to the personnel, it seems they had some gluten-free and vegetarian options.
    I must say though, that by looking at the menu, the food seemed quite expensive and not that special.

    Ratings:
    • 3/5: there are some options but it is very pricy.
    • 2/5: there are some options but not too exciting.
